# Specific services and safe connections

## Select actual services when input is incomplete

Do not require the user to provide a flight/train/service number. Preserve known origins, final destinations, dates, booked services and mode preferences. Research the applicable official timetable and recommend a concrete primary service plus later recovery service(s). Give operator, flight/train/service identifier, departure/arrival date and local times, boarding/alighting terminal/station/stop, reservation requirement, applicable fare and official booking/timetable URL. Where the operator does not number a service, identify it by operator + route + exact dated departure. A broad “09:00–10:00 take a train” window is not a substitute when actual timetable data are published. Do not invent service numbers or claim seats are available from a static timetable.

Research alternatives across relevant modes: local/express rail, metro, airport/intercity coach, public bus, taxi/private transfer, ferry, rental car/driving and connecting flight when geographically sensible. Present three or more genuinely useful modes/options where available, with door-to-door duration, walking/interchanges, baggage, fare, service cutoff and appropriate use. Do not cap the list at one or two, pad with unavailable modes, or override a booked/mode-specific primary journey. Unviable mode exclusions belong in research; show the useful choices in concise route comparisons.

## Preceding activities determine the earliest boarding

Calculate readiness from the END of the previous activity, not merely scheduled arrival at a station/airport. For an international flight connection consider disembarkation, immigration, baggage reclaim (including whether checked baggage exists), customs, physical terminal/rail/bus-stop distance, elevator/walking pace, queue to purchase/collect tickets, gate/security/boarding cutoff, food/rest if needed and delay reserve. Use dated official transfer maps and published cutoffs. Use conservative planning ranges where queues are unknown and label practical uncertainty briefly; never describe immigration duration as guaranteed.

For rail/intercity connections include alighting, platform change, vertical circulation, exits/terminal transfer, queue, baggage and boarding reserve. For hotel departure work backward from the latest safe boarding/check-in cutoff and include packing, checkout, retrieval of stored luggage and street-to-platform travel. For a museum/event/workshop include the real end time and exit travel; do not schedule a train while the activity still runs. For air-to-air consider independent tickets, domestic/international rules, rechecking and minimum connection times. An airline’s protected minimum connection time is not automatically appropriate for independent tickets.

Earliest safe departure = preceding end + required process/walking/queue/boarding/delay buffers. Choose a verified service AFTER that threshold, then a later backup. Check dates, timezone changes, last practical departure and hotel reception. Publish a short actionable note, e.g. "Take the 18:40 service; allow about 90 minutes for baggage collection and transfer. If delayed, use the 19:10 backup", only with researched actual services. Keep the arithmetic/internal assessment out of the reader prose.

## Unpublished or historical service data

Look for date-applicable original timetables/archives first. Future schedules not released and historical schedules not found are distinct statuses. State the exact limited fact briefly: "The timetable for that date has not yet been released" or "The original July timetable could not be found". Do not pass current service numbers/times off as historical. An explicitly provisional current-timetable example may be useful only with its actual effective date and a clear recheck notice; it is not the date-specific primary recommendation. Keep source-search attempts and detailed diagnostics in research. Continue all independent guide work and report the unresolved operational detail without inventing it.

## Internal structured audit

Maintain `research/TRANSPORT_PLAN.json` with `legs`, one per physical travel segment used on arrival/departure (and important interchanges), plus `considered_modes` for the main terminal-to-base transfer. Each leg has `id`, `role` (`outbound`, `return`, `transfer`), `mode`, `origin`, `destination`, `status` (`booked`, `recommended`, `unreleased`, `historical_unavailable`, `continuous`), `operator`, `service` or `route`, `source_urls`, `checked_on`. For timetable-based booked/recommended legs also store full ISO datetimes with UTC offsets in `departure`/`arrival`; never omit the date or mix timezones. Copy the actually recommended identifier and departure/arrival into the guide. Taxi/driving/unscheduled transit use `continuous`, a route and door-to-door duration, not a fictional service number.

When there is a preceding arrival/activity store `preceding: {type, ends_at, buffer_minutes}`. `buffer_minutes` maps process names to nonnegative numeric minutes; document derivation/source or assumption ranges in research. For `international_flight`, include `disembarkation`, `immigration`, `baggage`, `customs`, `terminal_transfer`, `ticketing_boarding`, `delay_reserve`; zero baggage is appropriate with no checked luggage, not an excuse to ignore queues. For other activities include relevant process/walking/boarding reserves. Earliest safe threshold must be earlier than the selected departure. For an unreleased/historical-unavailable leg include a nonempty `limitation` and retrieval attempt sources; no fake date-specific departure.

`considered_modes` is a list of `{mode, status: viable|excluded, route, source_url, note}`; explain exclusions without inventing geography. Research three or more options where practical; if fewer than three modes can sensibly be assessed, provide a source-grounded `alternatives_limitation` explaining actual geography/user constraints in this internal record. Multiple variants of the same train do not represent multiple transport modes. Mechanical checks verify data shape and arithmetic; an agent must verify service authenticity, actual applicability and physical transfer adequacy. These audit JSON records and field/status labels are not printed guide content.
